**Fixed: recordings stopping part-way through while the key was still held,
and finished dictations delivering nothing.** Every hold-to-talk recording
arms a two-minute safety timeout, in case the desktop never reports the key
being released. Releasing the key was supposed to cancel it and did not, so it
fired two minutes after that dictation had *started* — and stopped whichever
recording was running at that moment, with the key still down. If that landed
mid-sentence, the text so far was delivered while you were still talking and
the rest was never recorded. If it landed just after the key went down, the
clip was too short to transcribe and the whole dictation delivered nothing.
Each dictation left one of these behind, so the more you dictated the more
often it happened: rare in occasional use, most recordings in sustained use.
Releasing the key now cancels that dictation's timeout. Affected `hold`
bindings on every platform since 0.6.3; `double_tap_hold` and toggle bindings
were not affected.

**Fixed: starting the next dictation before the last one had appeared cut the
new one short.** On desktops where the compositor delivers the shortcut,
VoxCtrl's own paste ended whatever dictation you were holding the key for: KDE
reports a held shortcut released as soon as any key is released — the paste's
included — and does not report it held again. The change in 0.8.10 waited for
the shortcut to come back, which on KDE it never does, so the recording still
ended, a quarter of a second later. Finished text is now kept until the
dictation key is up and delivered then, in order. Nothing is lost; text from
the previous dictation appears when you let go rather than part-way through
your next sentence. Dictating one sentence at a time is unchanged, as are
toggle bindings and desktops where VoxCtrl watches the keyboard itself (X11,
evdev, Windows).
