**Fixed: snippets that worked on one speech backend and not another.** A
snippet trigger made of more than one word — `my address`, `sign off` — only
matched when its words were separated by a plain space. whisper.cpp, Moonshine
and the remote engines punctuate and capitalise what they hear, while Parakeet
largely does not, so the same sentence reaches snippet expansion in different
shapes depending on which backend is selected. A trigger of `my address`
expanded in `send my address now` but was silently skipped in `Send my,
address now.` — the same words, the same snippet, a different engine. The gaps
between a trigger's words now tolerate punctuation as well as spaces, so a
trigger behaves the same whichever backend produced the transcript. The words
themselves must still all be present and in order, so `myaddress` and `my home
address` are left alone as before, and single-word triggers are unchanged.

**Fixed: snippets expanding differently from one launch to the next.** When
one trigger was nested inside another — `addr` alongside `my addr` — which of
them won was decided by hash ordering, which is re-seeded every time the app
starts. The same sentence could expand one way today and the other way
tomorrow, with nothing having changed. Triggers are now applied longest first,
so the more specific phrase wins, and ties are settled alphabetically so the
result is the same on every run. This matches how custom vocabulary correction
and voice-command target matching already behaved.

> **Known limitation:** a snippet trigger has to begin and end with a letter or
> a digit. One written as `;addr` or `/sig` never matches, on any backend.

**Fixed: snippets not applying to the final transcript on whisper.cpp.** A
snippet trigger is often a short made-up word, chosen precisely because it is
not ordinary speech — and expansion matches it literally, so it only fires if
the engine spells it exactly as configured. whisper.cpp decodes a short
interim window and the finished utterance as two separate passes, and with
nothing telling it how such a word is spelled it could spell it one way while
you were still speaking and another way at the end. The snippet expanded in
the live text and the command overlay, then failed to expand in the text that
was actually delivered — which, for a snippet used to trigger a command, meant
the command was typed out as dictation instead of running. Snippet triggers
are now named in the recognition prompt alongside custom vocabulary, so their
spelling stays put across both passes. Parakeet and Moonshine were unaffected
and are unchanged.

**Fixed: custom Piper voices could not be selected, and played at the wrong
pitch.** The Piper voice picker only ever listed its built-in catalogue, so a
voice added to the voices folder could not be chosen at all and the only way
to use one was to overwrite a catalogue voice's files. Any voice in the folder
that has both its model and its `.onnx.json` config is now offered, marked
*(Custom)*. Playback also took the sample rate from the catalogue rather than
from the voice, so a voice recorded at a different rate — including one
swapped in over a catalogue voice — played too fast or too slow and at the
wrong pitch. The rate now comes from each voice's own config, falling back to
the catalogue only when a config cannot be read.
